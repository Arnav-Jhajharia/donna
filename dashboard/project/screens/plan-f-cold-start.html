Why cold start is a feature, not an edge case.

Most onboarding flows are a tax the user pays before the product is useful. Plan F's cold start is different: it's the same surface as day 100, just with the offers replaced by prompts. The ritual doesn't change shape as Donna learns — only the contents change.

By day 7, shadow has usually generated at least one real candidate. On day 7 morning, offer #1 is a real shadow, offers #2 and #3 are still prompts. On day 14, all three are real. The cold-start prompts retire as the shadow gets smart enough to fill their slots.

Day 1–3Three prompts, all cold-start. Focus on Living (person, season, time-to-stop).
Day 4–6Two prompts + one shadow candidate (usually a ping or open_loop inferred from the first answers).
Day 7–13One prompt + two shadow candidates. Prompts rotate through deeper Living (patterns, protected hours, people to avoid).
Day 14+Full ritual. Three shadow candidates per morning, ranked by promotion_criteria. Prompts only surface when shadow has <3 candidates above threshold.
WritesEvery answer writes to Living and spawns one LIVE attention (origin USER_EXPLICIT, no dry-run needed — authoring via prompt already counts as intent + dry-run).
TemplatesThree prompt variants per card, per slot. ~12 cold-start template bodies. Reused thereafter as the "add an attention from the ritual" path.
