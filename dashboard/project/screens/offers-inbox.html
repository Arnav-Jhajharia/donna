Why inbox is the right metaphor for offers.

Users already know how an inbox works. Unread is bold, read fades. Tap to open, decide, it disappears. Nothing about Donna's offers breaks that pattern — unless you want to make them ignorable on purpose (Plan E's inline shadow-band) or make them ceremonial (Plan F's three-per-morning).

The failure mode is email itself: unread counts that grow, offers that rot. Two mitigations baked in: every offer shows its age in rust if past 48h, and there's a bulk "archive old" for when the user checks back after a week away.

UnreadRust tint on row background. Strong visual signal. Decays to default on mark-read or open.
Age coloringAge in rust after 48h, oxblood "aging" badge after 7d, auto-archive at 14d with a quiet note. No shame, just decay.
Expand in placeOpening a row reveals the full dry-run card without leaving the inbox. Decide, and the row either turns into a live attention row (moves to /machinery) or archives.
Bulk actions"Accept all unread" is a real button — users who trust Donna batch-accept. "Archive old" collapses >7d offers with one tap.
Plan reuseB reuses these rows on the Sunday harvest page. E puts them inline on the main scroll. F never uses this surface — its offers arrive only through the morning ritual.
Empty state"nothing waiting. i'll keep watching." plus a link to /machinery. Empty-inbox feel — peaceful, not broken.
